HOA Committee Charter Template

A committee charter defines the purpose, authority, and operating rules of any HOA committee. Copy this 9-section template and customize it for architectural review, safety, finance, landscaping, nominating, or any standing committee.

What Is a Committee Charter?

A committee charter is a written document, adopted by the Board of Directors, that formally establishes a committee and defines how it operates. It answers the five questions every committee member and homeowner should be able to look up: What is this committee for? Who serves on it? What authority does it have? How often does it meet? And who does it report to?

Without a charter, committees drift. Members disagree about scope. Homeowners don't know whether a committee decision is binding or merely a suggestion. Boards lose track of what they delegated. A written charter prevents all of this by putting the rules on paper before the first meeting.

Every HOA standing committee -- architectural review, safety, finance, landscaping, nominating, social -- should have its own charter. The template below is generic enough to work for any committee type. Fill in the highlighted placeholders, have the Board adopt it by resolution, and your committee has a clear operating framework from day one.

Before You Adopt: Your CC&Rs or Bylaws may contain specific requirements for committee formation, membership, or authority limits. Review your governing documents first, then customize this template to comply with those requirements and your state's HOA statutes.

Committee Charter Template — Customize the highlighted sections
COMMITTEE CHARTER [Association Full Legal Name] [Committee Name] Committee Charter Adopted by Board Resolution on [Date] I. PURPOSE The [Committee Name] Committee is established by the Board of Directors of [Association Name] to [state the committee's primary purpose -- e.g., "review and approve or deny all requests for exterior modifications to properties within the community"]. II. TYPE OF COMMITTEE Type: [Standing Committee / Special Committee] Program Area: [e.g., Safety & Security, Architectural Review, Finance, Landscaping, Social Events, Nominating] III. MEMBERSHIP a. The committee shall consist of [5-7] members. b. Members shall be appointed by the Board of Directors. c. At least [1] member must be a current Board member. d. All members must be owners in good standing within the association. e. Term of service: [1 year / 2 years], renewable by Board appointment. IV. CHAIRPERSON a. The Chairperson shall be selected by [committee members / Board of Directors]. b. Term of Chair: [1 year]. c. A Vice Chair shall be designated to act in the Chair's absence. d. The Chair reports directly to the Board of Directors. V. AUTHORITY a. The committee is advisory in nature; final decision-making authority rests with the Board of Directors. b. The committee [does / does not] have authority to approve expenditures up to $[amount] without prior Board approval. c. All recommendations must be submitted to the Board in writing. VI. STAFFING a. [Management Company / Site Manager] shall provide administrative support to the committee. b. Staff members serve in an advisory, non-voting capacity. VII. MEETINGS a. The committee shall meet [monthly / quarterly / as needed]. b. All meetings are open to association members per [state open meeting laws -- e.g., "Florida Statute 720.303(2)"]. c. Meeting minutes shall be recorded and submitted to the Board within [7] days. VIII. REPORTS a. The committee shall provide [written / verbal] reports to the Board at each regular Board meeting. b. An annual summary report shall be submitted at the end of each fiscal year. IX. TERMS & CONDITIONS a. Members may be removed by majority vote of the Board of Directors. b. Vacancies shall be filled by Board appointment. c. This charter may be amended by the Board of Directors at any time. Adopted: [Date] Amended: [Date] _____________________________ [Board President Name], President Board of Directors [Association Name]

5 Example Charters: How to Fill In the Template

Below are filled-in examples of the Purpose and Authority sections for the five most common HOA committees. Use these as a starting point, then adjust for your community's size, governing documents, and state law.

1. Architectural Review Committee

Purpose: The Architectural Review Committee is established by the Board of Directors to review all homeowner applications for exterior modifications, additions, and landscaping changes, and to approve or deny those applications based on the community's published Architectural Guidelines and CC&Rs. The committee ensures visual consistency and property value protection across the community.

Authority: The committee has authority to approve or deny modification applications without prior Board approval, provided the decision is consistent with the published Architectural Guidelines. Denied applicants may appeal to the full Board within 30 days. The committee does not have authority to approve expenditures.

2. Safety & Security Committee

Purpose: The Safety & Security Committee is established by the Board of Directors to evaluate community safety risks, recommend security improvements, coordinate with local law enforcement and emergency services, and promote safety awareness among residents.

Authority: The committee is advisory in nature. It may recommend security expenditures to the Board but does not have authority to approve expenditures or enter into contracts. Emergency safety concerns may be reported directly to the Board President or management company outside of regular meeting schedules.

3. Finance / Budget Committee

Purpose: The Finance Committee is established by the Board of Directors to review the association's annual budget, monitor financial performance against the approved budget, review reserve fund adequacy, and provide financial recommendations to the Board.

Authority: The committee is advisory in nature and does not have authority to approve expenditures, sign contracts, or direct the management company. All financial recommendations must be submitted to the Board in writing. The committee shall have access to monthly financial statements, bank reconciliations, and the reserve study.

4. Nominating Committee

Purpose: The Nominating Committee is established by the Board of Directors to solicit, vet, and present qualified candidates for election to the Board of Directors at each annual meeting. The committee encourages owner participation in governance and ensures that the election process complies with governing documents and state law.

Authority: The committee is advisory in nature. It may not endorse or campaign for specific candidates. It shall verify candidate eligibility per the Bylaws and present the full slate of qualified candidates to the membership. The committee does not have authority to approve expenditures.

5. Landscaping / Grounds Committee

Purpose: The Landscaping Committee is established by the Board of Directors to oversee the maintenance and improvement of all common-area landscaping, recommend vendor contracts for grounds maintenance, and develop long-term landscape improvement plans consistent with the community's aesthetic standards and budget.

Authority: The committee has authority to direct the landscape vendor on routine maintenance tasks within the approved scope of work. The committee does not have authority to approve expenditures exceeding $500 or to modify vendor contracts without Board approval. All improvement recommendations must be submitted to the Board in writing with cost estimates.

Why Every Committee Needs a Charter

A committee without a charter is a committee waiting for a conflict. When there is no written document defining scope, authority, and reporting requirements, disagreements become personal instead of procedural. A charter lets everyone -- committee members, the Board, and homeowners -- point to the same written rules. It protects the committee from Board overreach, and it protects the Board from committee overreach. Most importantly, it gives homeowners confidence that committee decisions are being made within a defined, transparent framework rather than on the personal preferences of whoever happens to be chairing the meeting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common charter mistake is giving a committee authority it doesn't legally have. Only the Board of Directors can bind the association to contracts, approve assessments, or spend association funds above de minimis amounts. If your charter gives a committee spending authority, keep the limit low and require written Board ratification above that limit. The second mistake is failing to specify the reporting cadence. A committee that doesn't report to the Board regularly will eventually make a decision the Board disagrees with, and by then it may be too late to reverse. Third, don't skip the open meeting requirement. In most states, committee meetings that exercise decision-making authority delegated by the Board must be open to all members. Check your state statute and build that requirement into the charter.

How to Get Your Charter Approved

Draft the charter using this template, then submit it to the Board as an agenda item for the next regular Board meeting. The Board adopts the charter by resolution -- a simple motion, second, and majority vote. The adopted charter should be filed with the association's official records and made available to any homeowner upon request. If your Bylaws specify a process for creating committees, follow that process exactly. Some Bylaws require a supermajority vote or specify term limits that your charter must match. Once adopted, distribute the charter to all committee members and post it where homeowners can find it -- on the association website, in the community binder, or both.
